# ## 5.3.1 Research and portfolio management

In research and portfolio management, agentic systems can assist analysts and portfolio managers in processing large volumes of information, generating investment hypotheses, and producing structured outputs such as investment memos. Typical agent roles include:

+   - **Research analyst agents** that retrieve and summarize company filings, earnings calls, and research reports;
+   - **Macro analyst agents** that analyze macroeconomic data, central bank communications, and global news;
+   - **Quantitative analyst agents** that perform statistical or machine learning analyses on market and fundamental data;
+   - **Portfolio analyst agents** that analyze portfolios, factor exposures, and scenario impacts.

These agents can be orchestrated by a central coordinator that responds to high-level queries such as “Summarize the impact of the latest earnings season on our portfolio” or “Identify the key risk factors for a proposed position in a given stock or sector.” The coordinator decomposes the query into subtasks and delegates them to the appropriate agents, then aggregates their outputs into a coherent answer or document. The running research workstation is a concrete instance of this pattern: it turns a broad portfolio-manager request into a sequence of evidence retrieval, metric calculation, risk review, and memo synthesis steps.

# ## 5.3.2 Risk and compliance

A research literature on financial agent systems now exists and is worth reading before building: Xiao et al. [2025] organize a trading workflow as a multi-agent firm with analyst, researcher, trader, and risk roles; Zhang et al. [2024a] add multimodal market inputs and a tool-augmented loop; and Yu et al. [2024] use verbal reinforcement across a manager–analyst hierarchy. Read these for the architectures, and read them sceptically for the results: nearly all report backtested performance, and the leakage channels catalogued in Chapter 13 apply to every one of them. An agent framework that reports a Sharpe ratio without stating what its base model had already read is reporting a number about its training corpus, not about the market.
