The layers form a feedback system. Monitoring creates evidence; intervention limits immediate damage; concentration controls reduce the probability that one defect becomes a common-mode failure; and cross-border coordination updates the monitoring and intervention standards after incidents. The framework also clarifies responsibility: institutions remain accountable for the agents they deploy, while regulators require aggregate visibility that no single firm possesses.

# 14.5 The future of financial LLMs: Emerging trends

Earlier chapters introduced multimodal and advanced reasoning systems in general terms. This section avoids repeating that material and instead focuses on their specifically financial implications: temporal alignment, provenance, constrained decisions, and integration with an agentic control loop. The maturity distinction remains important. Multimodal extraction from documents, charts, and calls is already emerging; closed-loop multimodal agents and neuro-symbolic controls remain early; a financial singularity is a speculative thought experiment.

# 14.5.1 Multimodality as time-aligned financial evidence

A financial agent rarely receives one clean input. It may need to align a filing published at 08:00, an earnings-call transcript at 09:00, a chart image, an order-book snapshot, and a policy document last revised months earlier. The central challenge is therefore not merely feature fusion but synchronized, provenance-aware fusion.
