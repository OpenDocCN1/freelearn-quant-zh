# CoT reasoning

Chain-of-Thought prompting is, at its core, about making the model think out loud.

Instead of jumping straight to a polished answer, you ask the model to work through the problem step by step. This technique proves particularly valuable for quantitative financial analysis, valuation modeling, and risk assessment tasks that require multi-step reasoning processes [Wei et al., 2022b].

Effective chain-of-thought prompting typically follows structured reasoning frameworks that mirror professional analytical methodologies, such as:

+   1. Problem definition: Clear specification of analytical objective and scope
+   2. Data assessment: Evaluation of available information and data quality
+   3. Methodology selection: Choice of appropriate analytical techniques and frameworks
+   4. Assumption specification: Explicit statement of key assumptions and their justifications
+   5. Calculation process: Step-by-step quantitative analysis with intermediate results
+   6. Results interpretation: Context-aware interpretation of analytical results
+   7. Limitation assessment: Identification of analytical limitations and uncertainties

Consider the following DCF valuation:

+   Valuation Objective: Determine intrinsic value per share for XYZ Corp using discounted cash flow methodology.
+   Step 1 - Historical Analysis: Review 5-year financial performance to establish baseline trends. Revenue CAGR = 12.5%, EBITDA margin trend = 18.2% to 22.1%, Capex/Revenue average = 3.8%.
+   Step 2 - Industry Context: Industry growth forecast 8-10% annually, margin pressure from input cost inflation (+2-3% annually), technology disruption requiring increased Capex.
+   Step 3 - Projection Framework: 5-year explicit forecast + terminal value. A conservative approach given cyclical industry dynamics.
+   Step 4 - Revenue Projections: Years 1-3: 10% growth (market expansion), Years 4-5: 7% growth (maturity phase). Justify based on market sizing and competitive positioning.
+   Step 5 - Margin Analysis: EBITDA margin compression to 20.5% by Year 5 due to competitive pressure, offset partially by operational efficiency gains.
+   Step 6 - Working Capital: Assume 2% of incremental revenue based on historical relationship and industry standards.
+   Step 7 - Capex Projections: Years 1-2: 5% of revenue (technology investment cycle), Years 3-5: 4% of revenue (maintenance level).
+   Step 8 - Tax Rate: 25% effective rate based on geographical mix and current tax legislation.
