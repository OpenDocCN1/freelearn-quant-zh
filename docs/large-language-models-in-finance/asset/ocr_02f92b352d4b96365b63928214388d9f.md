The snippet below shows a typical temporal split setup: the model sees only data from years prior to the validation and test periods, mirroring the constraint that exists in production, where the model is always predicting into the future:

```
# Temporal split: Train on 2018-2021, validate on 2022, test on 2023
train_data = data[data['year'] <= 2021]
val_data = data[data['year'] == 2022]
test_data = data[data['year'] == 2023]
```

# 3.4.2 Training configuration

With a well-prepared dataset in place, the next step is to determine how the model will be updated during fine-tuning. Training configuration translates the dataset into learning dynamics: it defines how quickly the model adapts, how stable the optimization process remains, and how well the resulting model generalizes beyond the training data.

This subsection focuses on two aspects of this process. We begin with hyperparameter selection, where we examine the key controls that govern optimization behavior. We then turn to the training procedure itself, outlining how these choices come together in a standard fine-tuning loop.

# Hyperparameter selection

Choosing the right hyperparameters for fine-tuning is as much art as science, and financial NLP settings have well-established defaults that provide a reliable starting point. Let’s look at the four parameters that most directly determine whether fine-tuning converges stably and generalizes beyond the training set.

The learning rate is the most sensitive hyperparameter. Fine-tuning uses rates in the range 1e-5 to 5e-5—roughly 10 to 100 times smaller than those used during pre-training—because the goal is to adjust the model’s already-useful representations, not overwrite them. A learning rate warm-up ramp is used to protect the model during the first few hundred steps, when the newly initialized classification head is producing large, noisy gradients that could destabilize the pre-trained weights if applied at full strength immediately. The schedule below linearly increases the rate during warm-up, then linearly decays it for the remainder of training:

```
\eta_t = \begin{cases} \eta_{max} \cdot \frac{t}{T_{warmup}} & \text{if } t \le T_{warmup} \\ \eta_{max} \cdot \frac{T_{total}-t}{T_{total}-T_{warmup}} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} (3.6)
```
