# 1.7 Optimization objectives

The training objectives used for LLMs are fundamental to their success and directly impact their suitability for different financial applications. Understanding these objectives helps practitioners choose the right pre-trained model for their specific use case—whether they need bidirectional understanding for document analysis (MLM-trained models) or autoregressive generation for report writing (CLM-trained models). Additionally, newer alignment techniques like RLHF are crucial for ensuring model outputs meet the high standards of accuracy and reliability required in financial contexts. The success of LLMs derives from the design of their optimization objectives suited to their tasks.

In Masked Language Modeling (MLM) as used in models like BERT, the objective is to predict masked tokens using a cross-entropy loss:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{MLM}} = - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{M}} \log P(w_i|\text{context})$$ (1.14)

This compels the model to learn context-dependent representations [Devlin et al., 2019].

**Causal language modeling (CLM)**, as used in GPT and many recent models, trains the model to predict the next token:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CLM}} = - \sum_{t=1}^{T} \log P(w_t|w_{<t})$$ (1.15)

This approach supports autoregressive generation, ensuring coherent and contextually appropriate text [Brown et al., 2020], [OpenAI, 2022].

Beyond supervised pre-training, reinforcement learning techniques, such as **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)** and newer methods like **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**, are employed to further align model outputs with human preferences or specific instructions.

The goal is often to optimize a reward signal $r(x)$ or directly optimize based on preference data for a generated sequence $x$:

$$\max_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \pi_{\theta}} [r(x)]$$ (1.16)

where $\pi_{\theta}$ is the language model's policy parameterized by $\theta$. These methodologies help refine model outputs to be more helpful, harmless, and honest, aligning them better with user expectations.
