# 2.6 How to build an LLM from scratch

Building LLMs optimized for financial applications represents the ultimate expression of domain specialization. It allows for the optimization of every aspect of model design, from tokenization strategies to loss functions, specifically for financial use cases [Brown et al., 2020]. The decision to build a financial LLM from scratch is rarely taken lightly. It typically arises when fine-tuning an existing foundation model is insufficient: when financial terminology is poorly represented in the base vocabulary, when proprietary analytical frameworks must be deeply integrated, when regulatory requirements demand architectural control, or when specific financial workflows benefit from structural innovations.

The purpose of this final section is to examine what that level of specialization actually entails. We move beyond prompting and fine-tuning to explore the full lifecycle of constructing a financial LLM: curating and preprocessing domain-specific datasets, designing specialized tokenization strategies, selecting training architectures and optimization methods, defining financial-specific loss functions, and building the infrastructure required to scale, secure, and monitor such systems in production environments.

# 2.6.1 Dataset preparation and tokenization

If a financial LLM is to be built from scratch, everything begins with the data. As we've seen earlier in this chapter, the scope, structure, and quality of the training corpus directly determine how well the model will reason about markets, regulation, valuation, and risk.

Constructing such a dataset requires deliberate curation across the full breadth of financial knowledge, while maintaining rigorous standards of accuracy and professional integrity, which goes beyond simply assembling a large volume of financial text.

# Constructing the financial corpus

Financial corpus construction must balance breadth of coverage with depth of expertise across all major financial domains. The objective is to represent the different ways finance is analyzed, reported, and regulated.
