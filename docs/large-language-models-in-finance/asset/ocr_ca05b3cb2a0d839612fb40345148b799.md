LLMs help by:

+   - extracting key ratios and textual signals from credit memos [Mai et al., 2019, Netzer et al., 2019, Berg et al., 2020],
+   - mapping narrative risk descriptions into standardized tags,
+   - monitoring news and filings for early-warning indicators (e.g. legal disputes, regulatory investigations, downgrades) [Campbell et al., 2008].

A systematic setup might maintain, for each obligor:

+   1. a structured profile summarizing business model, leverage, liquidity, and collateral;
+   2. a stream of narrative events categorized by risk type;
+   3. early-warning scores derived from both structured and textual factors [Bharath and Shumway, 2008].

# Implementation sketch: Covenant extraction

Loan and bond documents often contain covenants that are crucial for credit risk: leverage caps, coverage ratios, restrictions on asset sales, cross-default clauses, and so on. Extracting these covenants consistently is a natural task for document NLP, but production systems need clause-level evidence, defined calculation rules, and legal review. Public contract benchmarks such as CUAD and ContractNLI provide useful starting points for extraction and document-level inference [Hendrycks et al., 2021, Koreeda and Manning, 2021].
