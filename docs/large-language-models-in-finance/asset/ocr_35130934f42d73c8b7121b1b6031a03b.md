T5 treats every NLP problem as a text-to-text problem, enabling a unified approach across diverse tasks. Its objective is formalized as:

```
$$\mathcal{L}_{T5}(\theta) = -\sum_{t=1}^T \log P_\theta(y_t \mid y_{<t}, x_1, \dots, x_{T'}),$$ (1.5)
```

where \(y_{<t} = (y_1, \dots, y_{t-1})\), \(x_1, \dots, x_{T'}\) is the input text, and \(y_1, \dots, y_T\) is the output text [Raffel et al., 2020].

By extending the strengths of GPT-style generation and incorporating BERT-like understanding, T5 demonstrates how pre-training on massive corpora produces highly adaptable, general-purpose models.

The distinctions among GPT, BERT, and T5 arise not only from their training objectives, but also from the building blocks they share. We, therefore, shift our focus from model families to the shared architectural backbone that underpins nearly all modern LLMs.

> **Case study — Earnings call assistant:** Choosing the right pre-trained model matters. A GPT-style model is best for *generating* the summary output; a BERT-style model works better for *classifying* management sentiment (positive/negative/neutral) because it reads the full transcript bidirectionally before deciding. A T5-style model can handle both tasks with a single architecture before each as text-to-text transformation: “summarize: [_transcript_]” or “classify sentiment: [_excerpt_]”.

# 1.6 Architecture and components

In this section we briefly outline the key components of modern Transformer-based architectures—the backbone of contemporary LLMs. The goal is not to provide a complete technical treatment (that is the focus of Chapter 2), but to build enough intuition that later discussions of fine-tuning, retrieval-augmented generation, and multi-agent systems are easier to follow.

Understanding the architectural components of LLMs is crucial for practitioners working with financial data because these components directly influence how models process, interpret, and generate text. The multi-head attention mechanism, for instance, determines how well a model can connect distant pieces of information in a long financial document, while the feedforward networks control the model’s ability to transform and combine features. By grasping these fundamentals, you’ll be better equipped to select appropriate models, optimize their performance for financial tasks, and troubleshoot issues that may arise during implementation.
