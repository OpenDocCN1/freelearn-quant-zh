Each stage should test not only task performance but also calibration, prompt sensitivity, security, operational resilience, limit compliance, and behavior under stress [Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System et al., 2026, National Institute of Standards and Technology, 2023].

+   - **Controlled change and continuous learning:** Any mechanism that changes prompts, retrieval data, tools, memory, parameters, or decision policy can alter system behavior. Changes should therefore be versioned, tested, approved, and reversible. Online or continual learning should be bounded by explicit risk limits, with revalidation triggers for material drift, new capabilities, or changes in intended use. A system should never be permitted to rewrite its own risk appetite, permissions, or control logic without an independently authorized process.

+   - **Cross-institution research collaboration:** Institutions compete on implementation but share an interest in stable markets and credible governance. Pre-competitive collaboration can support common incident taxonomies, stress scenarios, semantic challenge sets, third-party dependency mapping, and privacy-preserving measurement of aggregate behavior. The FSB's recent work emphasizes information sharing, organization-wide governance, lifecycle controls, and monitoring of market correlations and third-party concentration [Financial Stability Board, 2024, 2026].

# 11.4 Implementation architecture: From theory to practice

The three pillars define what must be governed; the implementation architecture defines where controls operate. The architecture has four layers, illustrated in Figure 11.1. Evidence and assurance flow upward from infrastructure and behavior to risk and accountability, while policy, limits, permissions, and escalation rules flow downward. The layers are interdependent rather than sequential maturity levels.

A practical architecture should also separate generative reasoning from irreversible execution. In many use cases, the LLM is best placed in research, interpretation, monitoring, or recommendation functions, while deterministic services validate calculations and enforce permissions, position limits, order types, and customer-impact rules. An LLM should enter a hard real-time execution path only after the complete path has met a measured, use-case-specific latency and reliability budget.
