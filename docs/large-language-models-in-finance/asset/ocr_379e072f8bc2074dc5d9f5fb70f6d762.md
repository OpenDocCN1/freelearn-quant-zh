practice, production-grade systems mitigate these failures by combining LLM-based extraction with symbolic consistency checks and retrieval-augmented verification against authoritative sources such as original regulatory filings [Gao et al., 2023b, Chen et al., 2023].

The ability to reason about numbers as real quantities, rather than as textual artifacts, defines one of the most active frontiers in financial AI. Models that lack this capability may remain rhetorically impressive, but they will remain fundamentally unreliable for financial analysis.

# 1.10.2 Interpreting financial data

Yet financial reasoning doesn't stop at getting the math right. Numbers only gain meaning when they're linked to the text and rules that define how financial information fits together. That's where interpretation begins. At the core, LLMs convert sequences of tokens into high-dimensional embeddings:

```
$h_i = E_{w_{i,:}} \in \mathbb{R}^d, \quad \text{equivalently} \quad H = XE \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}, \quad (1.21)$
```

where $E \in \mathbb{R}^{|V| \times d}$ is the embedding matrix, $w_i \in \{1, \dots, |V|\}$ is a token index, $E_{w_{i,:}}$ is the corresponding row, and $X \in \{0, 1\}^{n \times |V|}$ stacks the one-hot rows of the sequence. The lookup is a row selection, not a matrix product against the token ids themselves — the ids are indices, and multiplying by them would be meaningless. These embeddings capture semantic and syntactic information but not exact numerical meaning.

Financial reasoning demands additional structure:

+   • Accounting identities: Balance sheets must satisfy
+   
+   Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

+   • No-arbitrage conditions: In a frictionless one-period market with no arbitrage, an asset paying cash flow $D_{t+1}$ satisfies
+   
+   $S_t = \mathbb{E}_t^{\mathbb{Q}} \left[ \frac{S_{t+1} + D_{t+1}}{1 + r_{f,t}} \right]$.

where $\mathbb{Q}$ is a risk-neutral measure, $\mathbb{E}_t^{\mathbb{Q}}$ is conditional on information at $t$, and $r_{f,t}$ is the one-period risk-free rate known at $t$. Three qualifications are routinely dropped and all three matter: the cash flow $D_{t+1}$ cannot be omitted for a dividend-paying asset; the expectation is *conditional*, not unconditional; and the relation presumes frictionless trading. Equivalently, the discounted cum-dividend price is a $\mathbb{Q}$-martingale. This is a consistency condition a financial LLM's output must respect, not a general pricing formula.

+   • Cross-modal reasoning: Modern multimodal models (e.g., Gemini) can integrate text, tables, and charts, producing more consistent reasoning across data formats.

Thus, financial LLMs are increasingly built as *hybrid systems* rather than standalone models.

Once models can connect structured data with narrative context, the next step is using those insights to inform decisions.
