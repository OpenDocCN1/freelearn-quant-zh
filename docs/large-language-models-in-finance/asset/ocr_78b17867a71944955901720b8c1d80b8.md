> **Final-phase update: governance as a risk envelope**
> 
> The right question is not whether an agentic system produces a fluent trading narrative or a better headline backtest. The right question is whether the whole decision system improves after temporal integrity, costs, capacity, execution, stress, failure injection, governance, and replay are enforced. A governed agentic system may be economically valuable because it reduces volatility, tail loss, drawdown, operational error, or audit uncertainty, even when it does not dominate strong learned baselines on raw return or Sharpe ratio. This is why the chapter treats generative alpha as a hypothesis requiring evidence, while treating bounded authority and auditability as production requirements [Noguer i Alonso and Pacheco Aznar, 2026].

**Proposition 14.8.1** (Backtest admissibility under timestamped records). *If every prediction record* $r_i$ *is generated from artifacts available at* $t_i$, *the portfolio rule* $p_i = \phi(s_i, \mathcal{I}_{t_i})$ *is fixed before observing* $R_{i+1}$, *and transaction costs are computed from executable prices available after* $t_i$, *then the resulting backtest is free of direct lookahead through the signal-construction and cost-estimation channels.*

*Proof.* By assumption, $s_i$ is a function only of artifacts available at $t_i$. The portfolio rule is fixed before the future return is observed, so $p_i$ is measurable with respect to $\mathcal{I}_{t_i}$. Transaction costs use prices that would be available at execution after the decision time and are not used to revise the signal ex ante. Therefore neither the signal nor the cost estimate conditions on $R_{i+1}$ or later artifacts. As with all backtests, this does not eliminate selection bias, data-snooping, survivorship bias, or market-impact misspecification; those require additional controls and multiple-testing adjustments [Bailey et al., 2017].

# ## 14.9 Bounded autonomy as the right frontier

The frontier of financial agents should not be framed as a race from human decision-making to unconstrained machine autonomy. The technically and institutionally mature objective is *bounded autonomy*: the agent can plan, retrieve, calculate, and propose actions inside a formally defined action set, while external shields enforce limits and humans retain authority over material decisions.

Let $\mathcal{A}_{read}$ denote read-only actions, $\mathcal{A}_{write}$ denote state-changing actions, and $\mathcal{A}_{material}$ denote actions with direct client, market, regulatory, or balance-sheet impact. A conservative deployment path is

```
$\mathcal{A}_{read} \subset \mathcal{A}_{read} \cup \mathcal{A}_{draft} \subset \mathcal{A}_{read} \cup \mathcal{A}_{draft} \cup \mathcal{A}_{bounded-write},$
```

where $\mathcal{A}_{bounded-write}$ is permitted only under pre-defined limits and review gates. The important point is that the action set grows only after evidence of reliability, not because the model becomes more fluent.
