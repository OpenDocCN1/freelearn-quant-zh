# Customized financial instruments

Generative systems can assist in searching over payoff families that match a client’s objectives, budget, and risk constraints. Let $\vartheta$ denote contract parameters and $C_0(\vartheta)$ the time-zero cost. A simplified design problem is

```
\max_{\vartheta} \mathbb{E} [U (W_T(\vartheta))] \quad \text{subject to} \quad \text{ES}_q(L_T(\vartheta)) \leq \overline{L}, \quad C_0(\vartheta) \leq B, \quad \vartheta \in \Theta_{\text{legal}}, \quad (14.24)
```

where $\text{ES}_q$ is expected shortfall at level $q$, $B$ is the premium budget, and $\Theta_{\text{legal}}$ represents contractual and regulatory admissibility.

An illustrative payoff family is

```
$W_T(\vartheta) = (W_0 - C_0(\vartheta))e^{rT} + \lambda(S_T - K_+)^+ + \mu(K_- - S_T)^+, \quad \lambda, \mu \geq 0. \quad (14.25)$
```

Here $\lambda$ determines upside participation above $K_+$, while $\mu$ determines downside insurance below $K_-$. The strikes control where each feature becomes active. Equation (14.24), rather than the payoff form alone, determines whether a design is suitable: the premium, tail-risk limit, liquidity, counterparty exposure, and legal constraints must all be considered. The expression is therefore an interpretable search template, not a universal theorem about an “optimal” derivative.
