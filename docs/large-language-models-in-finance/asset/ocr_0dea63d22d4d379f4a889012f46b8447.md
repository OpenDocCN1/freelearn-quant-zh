# 5
Architectures and Applications of LLM Agents in Finance

LLMs have rapidly evolved from single-call completion engines into the core reasoning components of complex software systems. Rather than operating in isolation, contemporary LLMs are increasingly deployed within agentic architectures, where they interact with tools, data sources, other models, and humans in multi-step workflows. In financial institutions, this shift is particularly significant: investment research, risk management, trading, compliance, and operations all involve sequences of decisions and interactions that can be partially automated, but must remain subject to stringent governance and human oversight.

From a literature perspective, this chapter sits downstream of the rapidly growing survey literature on LLM-based autonomous agents and LLM-based multi-agent systems Wang et al. [2024], Xi et al. [2025], Li et al. [2024], Huang et al. [2024], Chowa et al. [2026]. That literature organizes agent research around construction frameworks, planning, memory, tool use, multi-agent communication, applications, and evaluation. The narrower contribution of this chapter is not to benchmark agent frameworks or train agent policies, but to translate those ideas into a finance-specific workflow-design and governance lens: how to decompose work, bind agents to tools, preserve evidence, route escalation, and retain human accountability.

This chapter provides a technical deep dive into the architectures, challenges, and practical applications of LLM-based agents in finance. We move beyond the one-off question answering and summarization pipelines introduced earlier, and focus on multi-step, tool-augmented systems designed to operate within real financial environments. Our aim is twofold: to formalize the notion of an LLM agent and multi-agent systems for financial tasks, and to provide practical patterns, failure-mode analyses, and implementation guidance for practitioners.
