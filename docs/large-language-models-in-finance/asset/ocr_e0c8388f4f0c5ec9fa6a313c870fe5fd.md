and evaluation design can matter as much as raw model size, and useful results are achievable across different resource levels.

In the next chapter, we move beyond adapting models to extending them with external knowledge, exploring Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) as a practical framework for building grounded, up-to-date financial AI systems.

# Further reading and resources

For readers who want to go deeper, the natural next references are LoRA for low-rank adaptation [Hu et al., 2022], QLoRA for quantized adapter training [Dettmers et al., 2023], DPO for preference optimization without an explicit reward model [Rafailov et al., 2023], FinBERT for financial-domain encoder adaptation [Araci, 2019], and FinQA for numerical reasoning evaluation in finance [Chen et al., 2021]. A practical reading path is to first reproduce a small FinBERT-style classifier, then repeat the exercise with LoRA or QLoRA, and finally evaluate the resulting model with numerical accuracy, temporal consistency, factual grounding, and production A/B testing checks.
