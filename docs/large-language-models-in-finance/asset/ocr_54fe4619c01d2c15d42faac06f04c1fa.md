# **FinCon: CVaR-aware portfolio agent**

FinCon coordinates multiple asset-level agents through a portfolio agent optimized for CVaR-aware objectives. Let \(w_t \in \mathbb{R}^d\) denote portfolio weights, \(\Delta p_{t+1}\) the price changes, and \(\text{PnL}_{t+1} = w_t^\top \Delta p_{t+1}\) the one-step profit and loss. Over a horizon of \(T\) steps, we define:

\(\text{PnL} = \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \text{PnL}_{t+1}.\)

The portfolio agent seeks to maximize:

\(J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}[\text{PnL}] - \lambda \text{CVaR}_\alpha(L), \quad L := -\text{PnL}, \quad (12.12)\)

where the risk term is applied to the *loss* \(L = -\text{PnL}\), not to PnL itself. The distinction is not cosmetic. Under the standard upper-tail convention, \(\text{CVaR}_\alpha\) is the mean of the worst \((1-\alpha)\) tail of its argument; applied directly to PnL it would penalize the agent for making large *profits*, which inverts the sign of the intended objective. Writing the risk term in losses is what makes (12.12) a risk-averse objective rather than a profit-averse one.

For optimization, the useful form is the Rockafellar–Uryasev variational representation:

\(\text{CVaR}_\alpha(L) = \min_{\nu \in \mathbb{R}} \left\{ \nu + \frac{1}{1-\alpha} \mathbb{E}[(L - \nu)_+] \right\}. \quad (12.13)\)

**What is and is not convex here.** The right-hand side of (12.13) is jointly convex in \((\nu, u)\) whenever the loss \(L\) is convex in the decision variable \(u\) — which holds for portfolio weights under a linear P&L map, and is the setting in which Rockafellar and Uryasev state the result. It does *not* hold when the decision variable is the parameter vector \(\theta\) of a neural policy: \(L\) is then a non-convex function of \(\theta\), and the composite problem is non-convex however the risk measure is written. For a neural policy, (12.13) is a *differentiable, sample-based surrogate* with a well-defined subgradient, not a convex program. Claiming convexity here is a common and consequential slip: it licenses convergence guarantees that do not hold.

Optimize \(\nu\) jointly with \(\theta\) on the sample objective

\(\widehat{J}(\theta, \nu) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[\text{PnL}_\theta] - \lambda \left( \nu + \frac{1}{1-\alpha} \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[(L_\theta - \nu)_+] \right), \quad (12.14)\)

Any minimizer \(\nu^\star\) is an \(\alpha\)-quantile of \(L\); when the distribution of \(L\) is continuous at that quantile the minimizer is unique and equals the usual \(\text{VaR}_\alpha(L)\). With atoms — which discrete scenario sets always have — the set of minimizers can be an interval, and any point in it is admissible. The \(\theta\)-gradient of the risk term is supported on the tail — it is weighted by the indicator \(\mathbf{1}\{L_\theta > \nu\}\) — which is precisely what makes it a CVaR gradient.
