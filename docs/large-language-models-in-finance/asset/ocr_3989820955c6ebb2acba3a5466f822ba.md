+   • Data leakage risk: Careful separation of training, validation and test periods is essential but not always extensively documented.

These caveats reinforce the message of Chapter 9: multi-agent LLM systems are powerful tools for research and hypothesis generation, but their path to live deployment must pass through robust simulation, conservative risk controls, and human oversight.

# 12.3 Risk assessment, fraud detection, and credit modeling

We now turn to applications where the primary objective is not trading, but detection, classification, monitoring, and decision support in risk and compliance. These settings differ from the trading systems reviewed above in two ways. First, the output is often an alert, a risk score, a credit decision, or a case-management recommendation rather than a portfolio action.

Second, the cost of an error is not only financial: false positives burden operational teams, false negatives can create fraud or anti-money-laundering exposure, and opaque model rationales can create audit and fairness problems.

The section therefore separates three layers that are sometimes conflated in informal discussions. A supervised detector or credit model produces the primary risk signal; an LLM may enrich the input features or summarize unstructured material; and a separate LLM-assisted workflow may help analysts investigate, explain, or escalate a case. Keeping these layers distinct makes the architecture more auditable: the real-time detector can remain calibrated and independently validated, while the LLM layer is evaluated for evidence quality, rationale faithfulness, and analyst usability.
