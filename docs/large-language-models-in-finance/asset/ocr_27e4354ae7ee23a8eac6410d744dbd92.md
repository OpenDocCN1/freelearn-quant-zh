# Introduction to Large Language Models

Natural language lies at the heart of human communication, and recent advances in artificial intelligence have given machines substantial new fluency and flexibility in generating, interpreting, and transforming text. In this chapter, we will explore the foundations of large language models (LLMs), tracing their origins from early statistical approaches to today’s deep learning–based architectures. You will learn how these models work, why their evolution matters, and how they are reshaping financial analysis, from automating regulatory filings to generating market insights. This foundational chapter establishes the core intuition you need before diving into architectures, training details, and governance frameworks in later chapters. Rather than going deep into all mathematical and implementation aspects (these are developed in Chapter 2 and Chapter 15), the goal here in this chapter is to answer three practical questions:

+   - What is a language model, conceptually?
+   - Why did we move from statistical models to deep neural architectures?
+   - What does this shift mean specifically for financial institutions?

We trace the evolution from early statistical approaches to modern LLMs, always grounding the discussion in financial use cases such as earnings call analysis, regulatory filings, and client communications. Along the way, we introduce the main families of models (GPT, BERT, T5, and their financial derivatives) and show how they fit into the broader evolution of financial AI. Technical details such as attention mechanisms, optimization objectives, and large-scale training are introduced at a high level here and are developed rigorously in Chapter 2.
