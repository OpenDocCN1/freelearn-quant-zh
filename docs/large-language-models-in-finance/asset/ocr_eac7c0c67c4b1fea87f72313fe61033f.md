# ## 9.2.3 Reward modeling approaches

The success of RL in LLMs critically depends on the quality of the reward signal. Because it is often difficult to define a precise mathematical reward function for complex objectives such as producing accurate, compliant, and risk-aware financial responses, learning reward models from human feedback has become a common approach.

A reward model \(R_\phi(x, y)\) is typically another neural network trained to score a model response \(y\) for a given prompt \(x\). In financial applications, this score may need to reflect several criteria at once, including factual accuracy, numerical correctness, regulatory compliance, clarity, and risk awareness.

In pairwise preference data, reviewers compare two responses to the same prompt and identify which one better satisfies the evaluation criteria. The reward model is then trained to assign a higher score to the preferred response than to the rejected one. Under the Bradley–Terry model, the probability that a preferred response \(y_w\) is selected over a rejected response \(y_l\) is

\(\text{Pr}_\phi(y_w \succ y_l \mid x) = \sigma(R_\phi(x, y_w) - R_\phi(x, y_l)),\)

where \(\sigma(z) = (1 + e^{-z})^{-1}\). Maximizing this probability is equivalent to minimizing the pairwise logistic loss

\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{BT}}(\phi) = -\mathbb{E}[\log \sigma(R_\phi(x, y_w) - R_\phi(x, y_l))].\)

The loss therefore depends on the difference between the two scores rather than their absolute level; regularization or calibration is needed to prevent the reward scale from drifting [Bradley and Terry, 1952, Christiano et al., 2017, Ouyang et al., 2022].
