# 12.9 Open questions and research directions

Several research questions remain open before financial LLM systems can be understood as mature production infrastructure. The first concerns robustness across regimes, asset classes, institutions, and structural breaks. Many reported experiments are encouraging, but they are often conducted on narrow asset universes, short windows, or static document collections. A stronger research program would evaluate whether an LLM-based agent that works in one market environment continues to behave sensibly when liquidity, volatility, macro conditions, or regulatory constraints change.

A second question concerns evaluation under realistic frictions. Financial benchmarks must eventually incorporate transaction costs, market impact, latency, adversarial behavior, stale documents, missing data, and changing portfolio constraints. A model that is accurate on static financial NLP tasks is not automatically reliable in a workflow where an incorrect retrieval, a hidden prompt injection, or a small numerical error can propagate into a costly decision. This motivates benchmark designs that combine task accuracy with evidence faithfulness, numerical validation, cost-aware simulation, and human escalation rules.

A third question is mathematical. We still need better abstractions connecting the statistical learning properties of LLMs with the economic and game-theoretic structure of markets. Memory, tool use, and multi-agent debate are promising engineering devices, but their stability properties remain incompletely understood. Finally, fairness, privacy, explainability, and auditability must be embedded into the architecture itself rather than appended as after-the-fact controls. These issues link the empirical material of this chapter to the broader governance framework of Chapter 11.
