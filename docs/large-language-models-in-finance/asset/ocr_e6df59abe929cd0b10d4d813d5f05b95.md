Step 9 - WACC Calculation: Risk-free rate 4.5%, equity risk premium 6%, beta 1.2, debt/equity 0.3, cost of debt 6%. WACC = 10.0%.
Step 10 - Terminal Value: 3% perpetual growth rate (conservative given GDP growth assumptions), terminal ROIC validation.
Step 11 - Sensitivity Analysis: Value range $45-65 per share based on growth rate (±1%) and WACC (±0.5%) variations.
Conclusion: Base case intrinsic value $55 per share with current trading price $48 suggesting 15% upside potential. Key risks: market share erosion, margin compression exceeding projections.

What makes this effective is not the math alone. It is the visibility of assumptions and transitions. Each step is defensible. Each projection is tied to context. Sensitivities are explicit. That transparency reduces the chance of confident but shallow analysis.

More advanced versions push this further. You can ask the model to solve the same problem using different methodologies before reconciling the results. Or you can break a large problem into smaller sub-problems, solve each independently, and then integrate the findings. Both approaches introduce internal cross-checking — something every experienced analyst does instinctively.

At this point, however, we move from strengthening reasoning mechanics to embedding prompting within the realities of financial institutions themselves.

# 2.4.3 Domain-specific strategies

Specialized prompting strategies for financial applications must address not only analytical complexity, but also regulatory requirements and professional standards. Here, prompting shifts from being a reasoning tool to becoming an institutional control mechanism.

# Regulatory-aware prompting

Financial analysis does not operate in isolation. It sits inside dense regulatory frameworks that shape how research is conducted, how risk is measured, and how conclusions are communicated. Regulatory-aware prompting ensures that model outputs remain compliant while preserving analytical integrity.

One approach is compliance-integrated prompting, where regulatory requirements are embedded directly into the instruction itself:

Conduct credit risk analysis in accordance with Basel III capital requirements, incorporating regulatory capital ratios (CET1, Tier 1, Total Capital) and stress testing scenarios as defined in CCAR guidelines. Ensure all probability of default estimates align with IRB methodology requirements and include appropriate documentation for regulatory examination.
