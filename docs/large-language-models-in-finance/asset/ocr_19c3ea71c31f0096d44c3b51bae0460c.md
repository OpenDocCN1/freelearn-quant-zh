# # 12

Mathematical Innovations and Empirical Evidence

This chapter connects the architectural and methodological tools developed in previous chapters with concrete applications and empirical findings from the emerging literature on large language models (LLMs) in finance. The previous chapters introduced core LLM technology, fine-tuning, retrieval-augmented generation, agentic workflows, and reinforcement-learning concepts. Here we ask how those ingredients are actually combined in financial systems: which models are trained on financial text, how memory and multi-agent structures are used in trading and research workflows, and what evidence is currently available for risk, fraud, credit, and benchmark tasks.

The chapter therefore has a different emphasis from a pure model-development or benchmarking chapter. Its purpose is not to rank every available model, nor to present new proprietary experiments. Instead, it offers a structured review of reported evidence and translates recent systems into a common mathematical language. This is essential in finance because model performance is rarely a single-number property. The same architecture may behave differently across asset classes, regimes, document types, regulatory constraints, and operational workflows. A useful financial LLM must therefore be understood simultaneously as a statistical model, a software component, and a governed decision-support system.

We focus on four main strands:

+   - Domain-specialized foundational models for financial text;
+   - Memory-augmented and multi-agent trading systems;
+   - Applications in risk assessment, fraud detection, and credit modeling; and
+   - Evaluation frameworks and emerging application domains.

Rather than presenting new proprietary experiments, we synthesize and formalize patterns reported in recent work, and discuss how they instantiate the design principles of Chapters 2, 3, 4, 5, and 9.
