# 4.1 The knowledge problem in financial AI

LLMs exhibit fundamental limitations when applied to knowledge-intensive financial tasks [Lewis et al., 2020]. These models encode knowledge in their parameters during pre-training, which makes them powerful but also introduces several critical challenges in financial applications.

+   - **Temporal knowledge decay**: Market conditions, regulations, and company fundamentals evolve continuously. An LLM trained six months ago lacks knowledge of recent earnings reports, regulatory changes, or market events.
+   - **Specialized domain knowledge**: Financial documents (10-Ks, 10-Qs, research reports) contain dense, specialized information that may be underrepresented in general pre-training corpora [Wu et al., 2023b].
+   - **Proprietary information**: Firms possess internal research, risk models, and trading strategies that cannot be incorporated into public LLMs.
+   - **Hallucination risk**: In finance, accuracy is not merely a quality metric; it is a governance requirement. Hallucinated facts about portfolio holdings, risk metrics, or compliance requirements can lead to catastrophic errors [Bohnet et al., 2022].

RAG addresses these challenges by grounding model outputs in retrieved, verifiable documents at inference time. Rather than relying solely on static parametric knowledge, the model can access up-to-date, domain-specific, and auditable information when generating responses. This shift—from memorized knowledge to dynamically retrieved evidence—forms the foundation of modern financial AI systems and motivates the architecture developed throughout this chapter.
