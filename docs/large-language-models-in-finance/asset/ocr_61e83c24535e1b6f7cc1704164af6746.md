Consider the following earnings analysis examples:

# Example 1: Company A (Consumer discretionary, $5B market cap)

Input: Q3 2024 earnings - Revenue $1.2B (consensus $1.15B), EPS $0.85 (consensus $0.82), EBITDA margin 22.5% (prior quarter 21.8%)

Analysis: Strong beat driven by market share gains (+150bps YoY) in core categories, with margin expansion indicating operational leverage from fixed cost absorption. Revenue growth of 8.5% YoY exceeds category growth (+5.2%), suggesting competitive positioning strength. However, working capital increase (+$45M QoQ) merits monitoring for inventory management efficiency. Raise FY2024 EPS estimate to $3.40 (from $3.25) based on sustained momentum.

Risk factors: Consumer discretionary sensitivity to economic cycle; inventory management during demand volatility; competitive pressure from private label expansion.

# Example 2: Company B (Technology, $15B market cap)

Input: Q3 2024 earnings - Revenue $2.8B (consensus $2.9B), EPS $1.15 (consensus $1.22), R&D spending $420M (+12% YoY)

Analysis: Revenue miss (-3.4% vs. consensus) primarily attributable to cloud infrastructure headwinds, with enterprise segment declining 5% QoQ despite product refresh cycle. Margin compression (EBITDA margin 28.2% vs. 30.1% prior quarter) reflects competitive pricing pressure and increased R&D investment in AI capabilities. Management guidance indicates H1 2025 recovery contingent on enterprise spending normalization.

Risk Factors: Cloud infrastructure cycle timing; AI investment ROI timeline; competitive positioning in enterprise markets.

These examples do more than present conclusions. They demonstrate reasoning structure, quantitative integration, and risk framing. That structure becomes the template the model imitates.

Few-shot prompting can be extended further. You might provide bull, bear, and base case analyses for the same company to demonstrate scenario reasoning. Or you might arrange examples from simple ratio analysis to full valuation modeling, gradually increasing complexity. This progressive layering helps the model internalize analytical escalation rather than defaulting to surface-level commentary.

Few-shot prompting teaches by demonstration. Chain-of-Thought prompting, by contrast, teaches by decomposition.
