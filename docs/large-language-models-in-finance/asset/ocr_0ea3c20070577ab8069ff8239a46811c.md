**TASK** Forecast the distribution of 20-business-day realized volatility for the specified equity.
**INFORMATION CUTOFF** 12 August 2024, 16:00 New York time. Use no observation, filing, revision, or label first available after this cutoff.
**INPUT** 512 daily observations of returns, range, volume, and point-in-time event indicators.
**SUMMARY STATISTICS** Recent annualized volatility, drawdown, robust trend, missing-data flags, and the three largest autocorrelation lags, all calculated from the admissible window.
**OUTPUT** Forecast median and 5/25/75/95 percentiles, calibration warning, and machine-readable units. Do not issue an investment recommendation.
**VALIDATION** The numerical forecast is produced by the time-series model; the language model may format and explain it but may not alter the values.

Figure 13.4: Finance-specific task prompt for a reprogrammed time-series model.

The prompt makes the target, horizon, cutoff, admissible inputs, output schema, and division between language and numerical computation explicit. A useful formalization is to write a reprogrammed forecast as

$\widehat{Y}_{T+1:T+H} = G_\phi (\text{LLM}_\theta (P, R_\psi(Y_{T-L+1:T}))),$

where $R_\psi$ maps the numerical history into LLM-compatible embeddings, $P$ is the textual prompt, $\theta$ is frozen or partially adapted, and $G_\phi$ decodes the forecast. For finance, every component of this expression must be timestamped. If $P$ contains a market narrative written after the forecast horizon, the benchmark is contaminated even if the numerical data are clean.
