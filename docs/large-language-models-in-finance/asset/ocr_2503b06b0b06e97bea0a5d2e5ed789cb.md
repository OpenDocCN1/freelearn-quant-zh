In the second, the action is covered by a policy certificate whose owner is recorded in the governance system. Since the action cannot execute outside these two paths, accountability is never assigned to the language model itself.

This framing is more ambitious than mere caution. It allows financial institutions to expand automation while preserving a clean separation between reasoning, execution authority, and responsibility. The agent may discover opportunities or assemble excellent evidence, but the institution must still decide which action classes it is willing to authorize.

# 14.10 Summary

This chapter examined the transition from predictive financial models to bounded autonomous agents. It explained why agency requires memory, planning, typed tools, governance gates, and auditability rather than model scale alone. It defined generative alpha as the factor-adjusted performance of AI-generated strategies or instruments while preserving conventional standards of evidence. It replaced unsupported universal convergence and collapse claims with more defensible optimization caveats, simulation-gap diagnostics, and a local spectral stability condition. It also distinguished deployed, near-term, and speculative technologies; presented a modern guarded implementation pattern; and developed an autonomy decision rule based on marginal benefits, costs, and risks.

The next and final chapter synthesizes the book's central lessons, identifies the capabilities and controls that should guide adoption, and closes with a practical agenda for building financial AI systems that are useful, accountable, and resilient.
