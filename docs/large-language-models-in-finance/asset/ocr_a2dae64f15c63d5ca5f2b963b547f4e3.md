**Proposition 13.5.1** (Point-in-time artifact correctness). Under D1–D5, the article scores with d(j) = d, the aggregate S_{a,d}, the normalized signal z_{a,d}, and the decision π_d are H_d^{PIT}-measurable, and the pipeline is temporally non-interfering: π_d(H) = π_d(H') for any two d-equivalent histories. Changing any future-only text, label, price, model component, retrieval document, or configuration cannot change the decision formed for date d.

Proof. By (13.4) and D1, every article assigned to d is available before the cutoff with fixed historical content. By D3 each component of A_{v(j)} factors through the historical prefix; by D4 the assembled artifact is operationally available before q_d; by D5 its application is replayable without mutable future state. Hence each score takes the same value in any two d-equivalent histories. Aggregation is deterministic in those scores and D2 entity tags, the lagged transform uses only strictly prior observations, and the pre-specified portfolio map receives only z_{.,d} and the point-in-time universe. Both properties are preserved under composition.

The proposition's substance lies in its premise, not its algebra. An ex-post model treated as a fixed constant is formally measurable with respect to every information set; measurability alone can therefore never convict a contaminated scorer. What carries the chronology is D3: the artifact must be generated by an auditable prefix-causal build. Freezing a future-trained object does not satisfy that premise. This is the precise sense in which the filtration condition stated earlier in this chapter must be read: it binds the provenance of the measurement apparatus, not merely the feature matrix it consumes.

# ## 13.5.4 Magnitudes: a controlled decomposition

How large are these channels? To answer that without confounding, I built a synthetic corpus in which the truth is known: 100 assets, 1,400 abstract market sessions, roughly 28,000 articles with simulated publication times, a 15:30 decision cutoff with 15-minute latency, and a return process in which sentiment impact begins the day after publication and decays over three days. A transparent shrunken word-tilt lexicon — every fitted weight inspectable at every vintage — is deployed walk-forward under a clean point-in-time protocol and under five named violations: a full-sample supervised oracle (A1) fitted once on all labeled articles including evaluation-period outcomes; a matched-size future oracle (A2) that keeps A1's temporal reach but exactly matches the clean protocol's training count; label admission before realization (B); full-sample normalization (C); and timestamp fiction (D), which ignores latency and cutoffs and rebuilds the entire chronology on publication-day stamps. Comparisons are paired on strictly common asset-days, with Newey–West statistics [Newey and West, 1987] and block-bootstrap intervals, and separately on each protocol's native support, where coverage itself becomes part of the treatment.

Every number here is produced by pit_experiment.py, shipped with the companion materials: python3 pit_experiment.py --seed 11. The script carries a self-test asserting the temporal non-interference property of Proposition 13.5.1: perturbing future-only labels must leave the clean artifact unchanged, and the test fails loudly if it does not. The world is simulated, so the truth is known by construction — which is the point, because the size of a look-ahead bias cannot be measured on real data, where the counterfactual is unavailable.
