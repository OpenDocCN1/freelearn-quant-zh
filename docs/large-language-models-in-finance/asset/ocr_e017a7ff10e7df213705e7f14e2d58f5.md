Conditional prompting is one way to do that. Instead of giving the model a single static instruction, you embed simple decision logic directly into the prompt. The model then adjusts its emphasis based on defined conditions. For example:

> If market volatility (VIX) > 25, emphasize risk-adjusted metrics and defensive positioning. If VIX < 15, focus on growth opportunities and sector rotation strategies.

Meta-prompting takes a slightly different angle. Instead of improving the answer, you improve the instruction. You ask the model to critique or refine the prompt itself:

> Given this initial prompt for equity analysis, suggest three improvements that would make the instructions clearer and more likely to produce institutional-quality research: [Initial Prompt]

This can be surprisingly effective. Models are often good at spotting vague phrasing, missing constraints, or unclear output expectations when asked directly.

Self-consistency techniques push analytical robustness a step further. Rather than relying on one reasoning path, you deliberately ask for multiple perspectives and then require synthesis:

> Analyze this investment opportunity using three different analytical frameworks: (1) Traditional value investing metrics, (2) Growth-at-a-reasonable-price (GARP) approach, (3) Quality factor analysis. Synthesize findings into a unified recommendation.

In financial settings, this reduces the risk of anchoring on a single framework. It also forces the model to reconcile differences across methods, which tends to produce more balanced conclusions.

# 2.4.2 Reasoning-enhancement techniques

Few-shot prompting and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning represent powerful techniques for improving the quality and reliability of financial analysis generated by language models. These approaches leverage the models' pattern recognition capabilities while providing explicit guidance on analytical methodologies and reasoning processes. Where reasoning quality often matters more than stylistic polish, this distinction becomes critical.
