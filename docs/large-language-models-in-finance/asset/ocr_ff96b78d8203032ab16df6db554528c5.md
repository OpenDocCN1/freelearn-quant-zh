+   2. **Reward model training:** A separate model is trained on human preference rankings to predict which of two outputs a human would prefer. This stage must come after SFT because the preference pairs need to contrast responses that are already plausibly financial in nature; comparing random outputs would produce a reward model that learns nothing useful about financial quality.

+   3. **RL optimization:** The SFT model is further fine-tuned using PPO (or a simpler alternative) to maximize the reward signal, with a KL-divergence penalty anchoring it to the SFT policy. This ordering is essential: the reward model must already be trained before RL can begin, and the KL anchor to the SFT policy prevents the model from collapsing into reward-hacking behaviors.

Among these stages, reward modeling is the key step that translates subjective human judgment into a signal the model can learn from.

# 3.7.2 Reward model training

The reward model is trained on preference pairs: for a given prompt, human annotators are shown two or more model-generated responses and asked to rank them. The example below illustrates the kind of discrimination the reward model must learn. Both responses contain the same numerical facts, but Response B contextualizes those facts—explaining why the Services segment growth matters for overall margins—in a way that reflects genuine analytical judgment rather than recitation. Training the reward model on thousands of such comparisons teaches it to assign higher scores to outputs that demonstrate insight, appropriate hedging, and clear structure.

The example below illustrates a typical preference pair used to train the reward model:

> Prompt: "Analyze Apple's Q3 2024 earnings"
> 
> Response A: "Apple's Q3 FY2024 revenue was $85.8B, up 5% YoY. iPhone revenue was $39.3B, down about 1%. Services revenue reached $24.2B, up 14%. Net income was $21.4B with EPS of $1.40."
> 
> Response B: "In Q3 FY2024, Apple reported revenue of $85.8B (+5% YoY). Growth did not come from the iPhone, which was roughly flat at $39.3B (-1%); it came from the high-margin Services segment, which reached $24.2B (+14%). That mix shift matters more than the headline, because Services carries a materially higher gross margin than hardware. Net income was $21.4B and EPS $1.40."
> 
> Human Ranking: B > A (B identifies the revenue mix shift, not just the totals -- and both responses are factually grounded)
