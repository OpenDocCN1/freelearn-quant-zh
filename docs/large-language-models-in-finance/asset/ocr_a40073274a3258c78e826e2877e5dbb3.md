# Ethics, Governance, and Compliance: A Comprehensive Framework for Financial LLMs

Financial large language models can widen access to analysis, accelerate research and compliance work, improve customer service, and support more consistent decision processes. These benefits are economically meaningful only when the systems are reliable, contestable, secure, and governed in proportion to the consequences of their use. In finance, a plausible but incorrect answer can become a recommendation, an order, a credit decision, a disclosure, or a control failure; governance therefore determines whether technical capability becomes durable value or unmanaged risk.

This chapter develops an integrated risk framework for the ethics, governance, and compliance of financial LLMs and agentic systems. It combines scope-limited U.S. model-risk guidance, cross-sector AI risk management, financial-stability concerns, and emerging evidence from controlled market experiments [Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System et al., 2026, National Institute of Standards and Technology, 2023, 2024, Financial Stability Board, 2024, 2026]. The framework is organized around three substantive pillars and a four-layer implementation architecture. Documented regulatory events are clearly separated from controlled experiments, hypothetical stress tests, and composite implementation vignettes so that illustrative material is not mistaken for historical fact.

In this chapter, we will cover the following topics:

+   - Why comprehensive governance is essential for beneficial financial AI
+   - Theoretical foundations and a three-pillar governance model
+   - What controlled market experiments do—and do not—establish
+   - A four-layer implementation architecture from infrastructure to accountability
+   - Documented, hypothetical, and composite governance cases
+   - Decision-linked metrics and continuous improvement
+   - Future challenges, open questions, and practical reference resources
