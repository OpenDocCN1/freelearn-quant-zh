The client portion covered suitability-constrained advisory support, behavioral-finance-aware communication, privacy-preserving service sessions, transparent escalation, and calibrated planning indicators.

The technical examples also clarified the appropriate division of labor. LLMs are useful for extracting candidates, comparing narratives, drafting explanations, and structuring review notes. Deterministic systems should retain control of identity, permissions, numerical reconciliation, policy enforcement, portfolio calculations, and transaction execution. Authorized professionals remain responsible for compliance dispositions, suitability judgments, and high-stakes client outcomes.

These applications may broaden the availability of selected services and lower the marginal effort of some routine workflows, but they do not automatically reduce total costs or improve access. Data integration, model validation, security, governance, human review, and exception handling can be substantial. The defensible claim is therefore conditional: institutions can improve responsiveness and consistency when the technology is embedded in controlled, auditable processes and monitored against concrete service and risk objectives [European Securities and Markets Authority, 2024].

# 8.5 Open questions

Several research and implementation questions remain open:

+   - How should retrieval recall, long-context utilization, and document-graph accuracy be jointly evaluated for high-stakes financial questions?
+   - Which calibration methods remain reliable when customer behavior, product design, regulation, and economic conditions change over time?
+   - How can one policy architecture encode jurisdiction-specific suitability, disclosure, and recordkeeping obligations without allowing stale rules to persist in prompts or model weights?
+   - What evidence is sufficient to show that a behavioral intervention informs rather than manipulates a client, especially for vulnerable populations?
+   - How should institutions measure aggregate risk when many advisory and document systems share the same foundation model, retrieval service, or IAM gateway?
+   - Which decisions can safely be automated, and which require an accountable human even when model accuracy appears high?
