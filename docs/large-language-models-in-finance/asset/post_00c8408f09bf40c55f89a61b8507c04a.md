## 7.1.2 Sentiment and narrative signals

Sentiment analysis of financial text has evolved from dictionary-based methods [Loughran and McDonald, 2011] to domain-specific transformers such as FinBERT [Yang et al., 2020, Araci, 2019]. LLMs further generalize this by capturing multi-dimensional sentiment and narrative structure [Hassan et al., 2019, Calomiris and Mamaysky, 2019, Lopez-Lira and Tang, 2023]. For example, a document may be positive about revenue growth but negative about regulatory risk or liquidity conditions. Narrative signals capture recurring themes in text, such as inflation pressure, litigation risk, refinancing pressure, or management confidence.

Instead of a single sentiment label, a production system may estimate a vector:

```
s_t = (s_t^{pos}, s_t^{neg}, s_t^{unc}, s_t^{reg}, s_t^{liq}, ...),
(7.2)
```

where components might track positive tone, negative tone, uncertainty, regulatory risk, liquidity concerns, and so on [Baker et al., 2016, Hassan et al., 2019].

The terms s_t^{reg} and s_t^{liq} deserve separate interpretation. The regulatory-risk component, s_t^{reg}, is intended to capture language suggesting exposure to new rules, supervisory actions, enforcement investigations, capital or reporting constraints, licensing uncertainty, litigation, or other legal and compliance pressures. For example, repeated references to a regulator’s investigation, a pending rule change, or a possible fine should raise the regulatory-risk signal even when the document is otherwise positive about sales or earnings.

The liquidity-concerns component, s_t^{liq}, is intended to capture language indicating difficulty in trading, funding, refinancing, redeeming, or meeting short-term cash obligations without material cost. In market-facing text, liquidity concerns may appear as references to thin order books, wider bid-ask spreads, limited market depth, forced selling, redemption pressure, or impaired access to repo and funding markets. In corporate or credit documents, they may appear as warnings about cash burn, covenant pressure, debt maturities, collateral calls, or reliance on emergency credit lines. Separating these dimensions is useful because a text can have a favorable business narrative while still containing economically material regulatory or liquidity warnings.

These signals are typically used as:

- features in cross-sectional expected-return or volatility models [Kelly et al., 2019, Freyberger et al., 2020],
- conditioning variables in risk models (e.g. to forecast volatility spikes) [Manela and Moreira, 2017, Baker et al., 2019],
- explanatory variables in post-trade analysis [Hendershott and Menkveld, 2014].

# Illustrative template. Not production code.

from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForSequenceClassification
import torch
import pandas as pd

model_name = "ProsusAI/finbert" # or an internal finance-specific model
tokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(model_name)
model = AutoModelForSequenceClassification.from_pretrained(model_name)

id2label = model.config.id2label

def score_headlines(headlines):
    inputs = tokenizer(
        headlines,
        padding=True,
        truncation=True,
        return_tensors="pt"
    )
    with torch.no_grad():
        logits = model(**inputs).logits
        probs = torch.softmax(logits, dim=-1).cpu().numpy()
    return probs

# Load headlines tagged with ticker and date
news_df = pd.read_csv("news_ticker.csv") # columns: date, ticker, headline
scores = score_headlines(news_df["headline"].tolist())

sentiment_cols = [f"sent_{id2label[i].lower()}" for i in range(scores.shape[1])]

for j, col in enumerate(sentiment_cols):
    news_df[col] = scores[:, j]

# Aggregate to daily per-ticker features
daily_sent = (
    news_df
    .groupby(["ticker", "date"])[sentiment_cols]
    .mean()
    .reset_index()
)

# 'daily_sent' can be merged with returns, factors, and risk metrics.

## 7.1.3 Event-driven and macro strategies

Unlike continuous forecasting, event-driven strategies focus on specific announcements or shocks. Event-driven and macro strategies revolve around discrete information releases:

- quarterly earnings and guidance updates [Beaver, 1968, Ball and Brown, 1968],
- merger and acquisition announcements [Mitchell and Pulvino, 2001],
- credit rating changes [Hand et al., 1992],
- macroeconomic data releases [Andersen et al., 2003, Savor and Wilson, 2014],
- central-bank statements and speeches [Nakamura and Steinsson, 2018, Gardner et al., 2022].

LLMs support these strategies by:

- reading earnings releases and call transcripts in near real time, extracting surprises, forward guidance, and risk disclosures [Davis et al., 2015, Larcker and Zakolyukina, 2012],
- classifying central-bank communications as more or less restrictive than expected [Hansen et al., 2018],
- generating structured event summaries that feed into factor models or scenario generators [Ke et al., 2019].

A typical pipeline for macro events includes:

1. retrieving historical event documents and associated market reactions,
2. training models to map textual features to realized price/curve moves [Bholat et al., 2015],
3. using LLMs to process new events into the same feature space in real time,
4. combining textual signals with order-book and flow information [Menkveld, 2013].

## 7.1.4 Multi-agent research workstations

Research at asset managers and hedge funds involves:

- screening instruments based on fundamentals, valuation, and themes [Asness et al., 2013],
- reading and synthesizing large volumes of text,
- building and maintaining models of cash flows and risk,
- writing investment notes and recommendations.

LLM agents, as introduced in Chapter 5, can orchestrate this workflow under human supervision [Wang et al., 2024, Xi et al., 2025]. A typical research copilot may coordinate:

- a retriever agent (RAG over filings, transcripts, research) [Lewis et al., 2020],
- a summarizer agent (company snapshot, key metrics, recent events) [Zhang et al., 2020],
- a risk agent (governance, legal, macro risks, ESG) [Berg et al., 2022],
- a thesis agent (draft investment thesis with pros/cons).
