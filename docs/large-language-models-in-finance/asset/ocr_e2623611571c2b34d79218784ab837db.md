# 13.5 Point-in-time text signals: auditing the complete scoring artifact

The timestamp on a text record does not timestamp the model that reads it. A 2008 headline is historical, yet the instrument used to score it may have been trained, calibrated, prompted, or retrieval-augmented with information from 2012 or 2026. The apparent historical signal then mixes two information sets: what was available when the text arrived, and what was embedded later in the measurement apparatus. A backtest can be chronologically sorted and still be impossible to have traded. The preceding section catalogued these leakage channels qualitatively and gave an operational protocol for closing them. This section sharpens that protocol into a measurement standard: a formal admissibility condition for the complete scoring artifact, a proposition stating exactly what a clean pipeline guarantees, controlled evidence on how large each violation is, and an artifact-level checklist that an auditor can apply object by object.

# 13.5.1 The complete scoring artifact and its vintage

A text signal is a composition,

```
X_j \longrightarrow \mathcal{A}_{v(j)} \longrightarrow s_j \longrightarrow S_{a,d} \longrightarrow z_{a,d} \longrightarrow \pi_d, \quad (13.1)
```

where X_j is the as-available article, \mathcal{A}_{v(j)} is the scoring artifact deployed at vintage v(j), s_j is an article score, S_{a,d} an asset-day aggregate, z_{a,d} a transformed signal, and \pi_d the decision executed at the close of day d. Any data-dependent arrow can import the future. For a sparse lexicon the vulnerable object is visibly a table of word weights. For a modern system it is much larger. At build vintage t, define

```
\mathcal{A}_t = (\text{tokenizer}_t, \text{vocabulary}_t, \text{base}_t, \text{head}_t, \text{calibrator}_t, \text{prompt}_t, \text{retrieval}_t, \text{code}_t, \text{config}_t, \xi_t), \quad (13.2)
```

where \xi_t records all stochastic state needed for replay and unused components are null. For a dictionary, the vocabulary and weights carry the substance; for a retrieval-augmented LLM, every entry can matter.

Admissibility is not a training-data cutoff. Let \rho(\mathcal{A}_t) be the earliest time at which the assembled artifact is built, released or licensed, deployed, and operationally available. In practice,

```
\rho(\mathcal{A}_t) = \max\{c_t^{\text{data}}, c_t^{\text{tune}}, c_t^{\text{build}}, c_t^{\text{release}}, c_t^{\text{deploy}}, c_t^{\text{avail}}\}, \quad (13.3)
```

the six stamps being the source-data cutoff, the fine-tuning cutoff, build completion, release or licensing, deployment into the scoring path, and operational availability to the process that will consume the score. The last two are the ones most often dropped, and they are the ones that bite: a model released in March but not wired into the research pipeline until June was not admissible artifact in April, whatever its weights knew. A model trained on data through 2010 but first released in 2012 is not an admissible 2011 artifact.
