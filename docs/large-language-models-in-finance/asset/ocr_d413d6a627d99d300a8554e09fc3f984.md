where \( r_t(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)}{\pi_{\text{old}}(a_t|s_t)} \) is the probability ratio and \(\hat{A}_t\) is the advantage estimate.

While PPO provides a principled way to perform this optimization, it is often complex to implement and tune in practice. This has led to simpler alternatives that achieve similar outcomes with less overhead.

# ### 3.7.4 Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) [Rafailov et al., 2023] is a widely used practical alternative to PPO-based RLHF. DPO avoids training a separate explicit reward model and running an online policy-optimization loop, recasting alignment as optimization over preference pairs. This simplifies the pipeline and can improve training stability, although it does not remove data-quality, specification, or reward-misspecification risks.

The key insight is that the optimal policy under the PPO objective has a closed-form relationship to the reward function:

\(\pi^*(y|x) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x) \exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta} r(x, y)\right)\) (3.18)

Solving for the implicit reward and substituting into the Bradley-Terry loss yields the DPO objective, which operates directly on preference pairs without ever materializing a reward model:

\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}(\theta) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x, y_w, y_l)}\left[\log \sigma \left(\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w|x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l|x)}\right)\right]\) (3.19)

where \(\beta\) is a temperature coefficient controlling how much the policy is allowed to deviate from the reference model, \(y_w\) is the preferred response, and \(y_l\) is the dispreferred response. Intuitively, the loss increases the likelihood of \(y_w\) relative to the reference policy while simultaneously decreasing the likelihood of \(y_l\), calibrated by the margin between the two log-ratios.

A more intuitive way to understand how DPO differs from PPO-based RLHF is to look at how the two approaches play out:

+   * **Simpler pipeline**: No reward model training, no separate RL loop—just one fine-tuning pass on preference data.
+   * **More stable**: Eliminates the policy gradient variance and reward hacking instability that make PPO difficult to tune in practice.
+   * **Lower compute**: Requires no online rollouts; training is as cheap as supervised fine-tuning on the preference dataset.
+   * **Limitation**: Relies on offline preference data; cannot incorporate live human feedback as easily as online RLHF with PPO.
