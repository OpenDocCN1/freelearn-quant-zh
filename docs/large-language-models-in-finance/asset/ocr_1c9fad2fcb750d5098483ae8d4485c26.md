A useful finding record contains the suspected issue, exact text span, active rule or internal policy, severity, rationale, and recommended reviewer action. Severity should be derived from explicit factors rather than from an opaque model-generated label. For example, an institution can combine the materiality of the claim, customer reach, evidence quality, disclosure gap, and rule criticality into a configurable triage score. That score prioritizes review; it does not declare the communication compliant or non-compliant.

Multi-label classification can support this process because one passage may raise several distinct concerns at once. However, probability outputs must be calibrated on representative, time-stamped validation data and monitored as rules, products, and communication styles change [Guo et al., 2017]. Automated edits should remain proposed redlines until approved by an authorized compliance professional.

# ## 8.2 Advanced document intelligence applications

The natural extension of single-document extraction is reasoning across a set of related documents. Financial analysis often requires comparing annual and quarterly filings, earnings releases, footnotes, policies, contracts, and market commentary across time. This section therefore completes the document-intelligence discussion before the chapter turns to client-facing systems.

# ### 8.2.1 Multi-document reasoning and synthesis

A multi-document system must solve four separate problems: selecting the relevant documents, aligning their entities and reporting periods, detecting inconsistencies or changes, and synthesizing an answer with traceable citations. A document graph is useful when relationships themselves matter—for example, when an amended agreement supersedes an earlier clause, a footnote explains a line item, or a risk factor changes across reporting periods.
