+   - **Technical expertise**: Junior analyst, Senior analyst, Portfolio manager, Chief Investment Officer, Risk manager, or Compliance officer
+   - **Institutional perspective**: Buy-side equity research, sell-side research, commercial banking, investment banking, regulatory examination, or academic research

For example:

```
Respond as a senior buy-side equity research analyst with 10+ years
experience in healthcare sector analysis, preparing investment
recommendations for a \$2B long-only equity fund with value-oriented
investment philosophy.
```

Constraints further anchor the output. In finance, these usually include regulatory compliance requirements, defined risk parameters, quantitative precision standards, and reference to professional guidelines:

+   - **Regulatory compliance**: Specify applicable regulations (SEC, FINRA, Basel, MiFID II) and required disclosures
+   - **Risk parameters**: Define risk tolerance, concentration limits, and stress testing requirements
+   - **Quantitative standards**: Specify precision requirements, confidence intervals, and statistical significance thresholds
+   - **Professional standards**: Reference industry best practices (CFA Institute, GARP) and ethical guidelines

Stating these constraints explicitly helps prevent outputs that are technically correct but institutionally unusable.

Finally, specifying the output format makes the response easier to integrate into existing workflows. Clear structure reduces post-processing and improves consistency across analyses. A well-defined format might look like this:

```
OUTPUT FORMAT:
- Executive Summary (2-3 sentences, key recommendation)
- Quantitative Analysis (formatted tables, 2 decimal places)
- Risk Assessment (probability ranges, scenario analysis)
- Regulatory Considerations (compliance notes, required disclosures)
- Limitations and Assumptions (explicit uncertainty quantification)
```

When prompts are structured this way, they move from being instructions to being specifications. That distinction becomes critical in financial environments.
