# 4.10 RAG evaluation

Once a RAG system has been built, the next question is how to know whether it is good enough. In a financial setting, the question matters more than usual: a wrong answer about capital requirements, a fabricated quote from an earnings call, or a confidently stated but unsupported claim about a regulation can have direct legal and reputational consequences. Evaluation is therefore not an optional final step but the mechanism by which a RAG pipeline becomes deployable in a regulated environment.

We distinguish three layers of evaluation in this section. **Retrieval metrics** (Recall@k, Precision@k, MRR, NDCG) measure whether the right evidence is being surfaced and how well it is ranked. **Generation metrics** (faithfulness, answer relevancy, context relevancy) measure whether the model’s response actually uses that evidence and addresses the question. **End-to-end and protocol-level evaluation** ties these together with a reproducible pipeline and, for regulated use cases, expert review and explicit go/no-go criteria. The retrieval metrics in particular are familiar from classical information retrieval, but their role here is broader: in a RAG system, they are not just IR diagnostics but proxies for the upper bound on generation quality, since the model can only ground its answer in passages that retrieval has actually surfaced.

# 4.10.1 Retrieval metrics

The metrics in this subsection are deliberately compact, since they are standard in the IR literature. The point of restating them is to fix notation and to make explicit how each one is read in a RAG context.

**Recall@k** measures the proportion of relevant documents that appear in the top-_k_ retrieved results. In a RAG system it answers the question: “did retrieval at least give the model a chance to ground its answer?” A low Recall@k caps the achievable answer quality regardless of how good the generation step is.

$$ \text{Recall@}k = \frac{|\text{Relevant} \cap \text{Retrieved@}k|}{|\text{Relevant}|} \quad (4.15) $$

**Precision@k** measures the proportion of the top-_k_ retrieved documents that are actually relevant. It controls how much noise the model has to read through; low precision typically translates into longer prompts, higher cost, and a higher rate of distracted or hallucinated answers.

$$ \text{Precision@}k = \frac{|\text{Relevant} \cap \text{Retrieved@}k|}{k} \quad (4.16) $$
