where the terms penalize negative probabilities, excessive ratios, and mathematical constraint violations, respectively.

Temporal consistency losses promote coherence across related time periods. Financial reasoning is inherently longitudinal; predictions about the same entity should not shift arbitrarily across adjacent quarters or reporting cycles. This can be expressed as:

$$\mathcal{L}_{temporal} = \sum_{t, t'} w(|t - t'|) \cdot \text{Distance}(\text{Prediction}_t, \text{Prediction}_{t'})$$ (2.43)

where \(w(|t - t'|)\) represents a weighting function that emphasizes consistency for temporally proximate predictions.

Together, these finance-specific objectives help align the training process with the structural realities of financial analysis rather than relying solely on surface-level language patterns.

# ## 2.6.3 Scaling and infrastructure requirements

Scaling financial language models from research prototypes to production systems requires more than successful training. It demands deliberate attention to infrastructure and operational constraints specific to financial environments. The supporting architecture must accommodate both development and live workloads while satisfying professional standards.

# ### Computational infrastructure

Infrastructure design must accommodate peak training workloads while remaining operationally efficient and regulatorily sound. In practice, GPU cluster architectures for financial applications emphasize high memory capacity, fast interconnects, and redundancy to ensure reliability. Large-scale training runs often extend over weeks, placing sustained demands on power, cooling, and system maintenance.
